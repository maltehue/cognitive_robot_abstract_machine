The calibrated receiver perception and receiver-conditioned bank from `ff7ae54522` remain the basis. The stopping tolerance remains **0.01 receiver fill**. This update adds:

- Observation-conditioned flowing/stopped state inference within the physically permitted hysteresis interval, allowing perception to recognize a restarted stream without forcing a new onset estimate.
- Controller capacity weighted by the bank's non-stall probability. With the default stall probability of 0.2, physical capacity 0.8/s becomes expected capacity 0.64/s before smoothing.
- Rejection of trickle-flow configurations that the controller equation cannot represent; the demo no longer enables trickle learning.
- A 0.1-second live model smoothing time constant, applying about 95% of a correction within 0.3 seconds.
- One combined world notification for model corrections and the corresponding fill state.
- Latest-only camera and perception-reading queues to avoid processing stale observations under load.

The global motion/jerk limits, return-speed cap and forecast default are unchanged. The offline controller probe now supports consecutive cycles that retain commanded velocity and acceleration; this is evaluation support, not a new robot stop override. Broad flow-rate learning is not enabled.

## What the offline tests support

Eleven accepted recordings were evaluated. Frozen visible-flow forecast error improved after the expected-capacity fix from **3.78 to 3.10 percentage points at 1.5 seconds**, and **5.49 to 4.61 points at 3 seconds**. The earlier model without state inference still had lower aggregate forecast error, so forecast magnitude remains an open issue.

On the newest bag's second burst, consecutive controller replay reaches sustained backward commands above 3°/s **0.29–0.35 seconds earlier** with the current bank than with the model before state inference. The 3-second forecast adds **25–50 ms** of improvement in this measure and reduces renewed forward travel in the tested window.

The previously similar first commands were already limited by maximum braking jerk. Independent snapshots reset acceleration before every solve and therefore missed the accumulated response over consecutive cycles. Doubling jerk limits gave little additional benefit and increased forward travel in the tested sequence; keep the existing limits.

These are offline command and prediction results. They do not demonstrate a counterfactual final received mass, actual stop time or spill rate. Recorded perception and model corrections cannot reproduce what the camera would see after a different motion.
